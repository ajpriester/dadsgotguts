Two marathons. One dad. Some guts.

I'm 52. My body is less "marathon runner" and more "drives to the letterbox". I'm carrying a few extra kilos, mostly in the shape of croissants, and my knees make a noise when I stand up. In 2027 I'm running Tokyo and New York anyway, with two of my children, who have 30 years, all of their cartilage and none of my love handles on me. We're raising $50,000 for Keep a Child Alive.

The bet: if I don't finish both races, I donate double whatever we raise. If I don't survive them, my estate does. Either way, the kids win.

Questions

Frequently asked

Will you actually die?

Statistically, very unlikely. Emotionally, around km 35, yes. I've had a full medical and my GP has cleared me, with a look on his face I'm still thinking about.

Why should I donate if you're going to finish?

Because the money goes to children who need it either way. The bet only decides whether it goes once or twice.

What counts as "finishing"?

Crossing the official finish line of both races inside the cut-off. Crawling counts. Being carried does not.

Is my donation tax deductible?

Keep a Child Alive is a US 501(c)(3) charity, so donations get a US receipt. Australian donors can't currently claim a tax deduction. I'm working with KCA on an Australian option and will update this page if one opens up.

Why are your children doing this?

For the cause, and to make sure someone is there to film it when I sit down on the Brooklyn Bridge.

The pledge

I, Adam Joshua Priester, undertake that if I do not complete both the Tokyo Marathon (7 March 2027) and the TCS New York City Marathon (7 November 2027), I will donate to Keep a Child Alive an amount equal to the total raised through this campaign, in addition to the funds already raised, within 90 days of the New York Marathon. If I am unable to do so, I direct my executors to make that payment from my estate.
